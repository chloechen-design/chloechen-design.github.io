Overview

FTL connects businesses shipping goods from China with logistics services across Europe. I helped reorganise its website around customer needs, then led the wireframes and responsive UI through to development handoff.

Background

A website to support business development

FTL's services covered customs, warehousing, transport and last-mile delivery. The CEO needed the website to explain this network to prospective clients, including large merchants shipping goods into Europe.

The existing site already contained a broad range of company and service pages. The redesign needed to make that offer easier to navigate and keep the content maintainable for Marketing & Communication.

Problem

Visitors had to work out where they fitted

The business brief and review of the old site pointed to a navigation problem: a visitor could see FTL's capabilities without finding a clear route for their own shipment or business.

  1. Several audiences, different questionsIndividual senders, wholesalers, logistics partners and e-commerce businesses needed different starting points.
  2. Services needed contextVisitors needed to understand how customs, storage and delivery could fit together in their own logistics chain.
  3. A large scope for a small teamThe redesign covered multiple pages, languages and screen sizes within an approximately three-month window.
Process

Three decisions shaped the redesign

01Start with the customer's situation

The CEO identified four existing customer groups. We used them as entry points into the service offer. I worked with another UX designer on the site structure; they primarily organised the site tree, while I led its translation into wireframes and UI.

01Individual sender

A parcel to send

02Wholesaler

Goods to move and store

03Logistics partner

A European service network

04E-commerce business

Orders to fulfil and deliver

These were stakeholder-defined customer groups, used to organise content.

02Give the homepage a clear sequence

The homepage introduces the offer, helps visitors choose a solution, presents the network and company information, then provides a contact route. Detailed service explanations sit on the relevant solution pages.

  1. 01
    Understand the offer

    A short introduction to FTL's logistics capabilities.

  2. 02
    Find a relevant solution

    Entry points connected to customer contexts.

  3. 03
    Explore the network

    Partners, company information and operational coverage.

  4. 04
    Start a conversation

    Contact information and enquiry routes.

03Design and hand off reusable modules

I developed shared navigation, buttons, selection states, forms and content modules for desktop and mobile. Approved modules went to the internal Webflow team progressively, allowing design and development to move forward within the available time.

Impact

A complete website, delivered and launched

The internal development team built and launched the multilingual website in Webflow. Marketing & Communication supplied the content and continued updating it through the CMS.

My contribution covered the wireframes, full responsive UI and design handoff, alongside collaboration on the site structure. The shared page modules connected the customer entry points to a consistent set of solution and contact pages.

The website was later taken offline following the company's closure. Post-launch analytics are unavailable, so the results here describe delivery rather than a measured conversion increase.

Reflections

What I would carry forward

Connect every page to a customer question

Organising the offer by customer context gave the architecture a clear starting point. Today, I would test those entry labels with prospective clients to see whether they choose the intended route.

Plan measurement alongside delivery

For a new iteration, I would define solution-page visits, contact starts and completed enquiries before launch, then combine those signals with sales feedback.
