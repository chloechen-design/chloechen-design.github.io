Overview

One platform, several moments of understanding.

Esendeo helps e-commerce businesses compare carriers, prepare shipments, manage deliveries and track performance. My work connected its website, visual identity, email communication and sales materials so that each touchpoint explained the same service in its own context.

01 / Challenge

The offer had to make sense before the interface could earn trust.

Esendeo brings several carrier and shipping activities into one service. A visitor could arrive as a shop, an e-commerce team, a wholesaler or a logistics operator; each needed a different reason to explore the same platform. The project also called for a visual language that could travel from digital pages to recurring commercial material.

Design question

How could someone recognise their own logistics job, understand what Esendeo does, and meet the same clear identity later in an email or sales conversation?

02 / Design decisions

Three decisions hold the experience together.

01 ↗

Start with a business situation.

Show who the service is for before asking visitors to learn a feature catalogue. The homepage points to shops, e-commerce, wholesalers and logistics teams, while solution pages speak to specific operating needs.

See the website evidence ↓
02 ✳

Make an expressive brand usable as a system.

Keep a stable typographic hierarchy and deep teal for reading; use bright colors as flexible accents across formats. The choice preserves personality without making every page start from a new visual rule.

Inspect the visual system ↓
03 →

Give each message one job.

A welcome email helps a customer get started. A feature email shows what to try next. Sales material supports a conversation. They share a voice, but not an identical composition.

Compare the formats ↓
03 / Website

From “who is this for?” to “how does it work?”

The homepage introduces the service, branches into four business contexts, then explains an operational sequence: compare a carrier, ship, manage and track. A solution page can open with a more precise use case and still return to the same product capabilities.

04 / Visual system

The identity changes expression, not its rules.

The design files use Satoshi, a deep teal anchor and a broad palette with lighter steps. Rather than using every color on every screen, the system has a quiet reading layer and expressive moments for differentiation.

05 / Communication

A shared voice, with a different task in each channel.

Website content explains the offer. Lifecycle emails orient customers. Sales slides make the offer usable in conversation. A coherent brand needed all three to stay recognisable while doing distinct jobs.

What I would validate next

Test whether visitors find the right sector page without help, whether they can explain the compare → ship → manage → track model, and whether email recipients understand their next action. Track solution-page journeys and qualified enquiries after measurement is set up.

The available files do not establish production adoption, conversion change or email performance. The counts above describe design artifacts, not business results.
